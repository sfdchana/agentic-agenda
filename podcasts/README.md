# Podcasts

Low-energy way to keep marinating in the material — good for hard days, commutes,
chores. Curated for *my* lane (AI engineering + clean data for AI), not a generic
list. Search any of these by name in Spotify / Apple Podcasts.

## Start here (the two to actually begin with)

- **Latent Space** — swyx (Shawn Wang) & Alessio Fanelli. The definitive "AI
  engineer" podcast: agents, evals, RAG, inference, shipping to production. This
  is the single closest match to my whole plan. https://www.latent.space/
- **Practical AI** — Chris Benson & Daniel Whitenack (Changelog). The
  application layer: MLOps, evaluation harnesses, the unglamorous parts of
  actually shipping AI. Grounded, builder-focused. https://changelog.com/practicalai

## AI engineering — agents & evals (Months 1, 3, 4)

- **The Cognitive Revolution** — Nathan Labenz. One of the few shows that goes
  deep on real model *evaluations* and capabilities, from a builder's POV — pairs
  perfectly with the Month 4 evals work. https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/
- **TWIML AI Podcast** (This Week in ML & AI) — Sam Charrington. The deepest
  archive in the field; focused single-guest technical interviews on ML research,
  MLOps, and operationalizing AI. https://twimlai.com/podcast/

## Data engineering (Month 2 — the clean-data lane)

- **Data Engineering Podcast** — Tobias Macey. Behind-the-scenes on the tools,
  techniques, and hard parts of data engineering. Directly the Month 2 muscle.
  https://www.dataengineeringpodcast.com/
- **The Data Engineering Show** — practitioners going beyond theory on their
  real day-to-day pipeline problems (has covered RAG + agent automation too).
  https://podcasts.fame.so/the-data-engineering-show

## Practical LLM + RAG (Months 3 & 5)

- **DataTalks.Club Podcast** — practical LLM engineering and RAG: prompting,
  evaluation, real-world workflows. Good for the classification + retrieval
  months. https://datatalks.club/podcast

## For depth / big picture (any time, esp. hard days)

- **Dwarkesh Podcast** — long, deep conversations with frontier researchers.
  More "where is this all going" than how-to — good for perspective and staying
  motivated about *why* this lane matters. https://www.dwarkesh.com/

---

**How to use these:** don't treat them as study (no notes required). They're for
*passive immersion* — hearing the vocabulary (evals, RAG, tool-calling, context
windows) used casually by practitioners makes it feel normal faster. If a line
sparks something, drop it in `../journal/ideas.md`.
